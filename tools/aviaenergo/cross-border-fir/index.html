Evidence and publication control

Every claim is tied to a dated publication. A posted AIP is not proof that a rule is still effective today.

What does “cross-border trace” mean?

1. Locate the FIR boundary fixEgypt AIP ENR 3.1 identifies an ATS route endpoint as FIR BDRY.
2. Walk the Egyptian airwayFollow the published sequence of fixes and source route restrictions.
3. Match the adjacent publicationCross-check coordinates, route designation and next published fixes from the neighboring State.
4. Identify the statusFull fragment evidence, fix-match with different designation, crossing procedure only, historical contingency, or no verification.
5. Stop at the evidence boundaryNever silently extend an airway to a guessed point. Live clearance and current restrictions are outside this training product.

What this version does not claim

Accuracy comes from conservative claims, not from coloring every point green.

A same-name waypoint does not prove a single continuous ATS route.
A published radio procedure does not provide a full sequence of neighboring waypoints.
The Sudan contingency is a dated historical warning, not a current blanket airspace status.
Unknown means not yet verified, not closed or forbidden.
The internal graph may treat an airway sector both ways for educational connectivity even where operational use is one-way.
Operational rule

Always verify the effective State AIPs, ICAO route restrictions, current NOTAM, AUP/UUP if applicable, operator flight-plan validation, and ATC coordination before dispatch.
