2. What is inside the AIP?

The AIP itself has three major parts — not six and not four.

AIP STRUCTURE

GEN GENERAL

National rules and common reference information.

  • GEN 0 Preface and publication records
  • GEN 1 National regulations; entry, passengers, crew, cargo; differences
  • GEN 2 Codes, abbreviations and units
  • GEN 3 Aeronautical services
  • GEN 4 Aerodrome and air navigation charges

ENR EN-ROUTE

Information for using airspace and flying between airports.

  • ENR 1 Rules and procedures
  • ENR 2 Air traffic services airspace
  • ENR 3 ATS routes
  • ENR 4 Navigation aids
  • ENR 5 Navigation warnings
  • ENR 6 En-route charts

AD AERODROMES

Airport information for operational use.

  • AD 1 Aerodromes: introduction
  • AD 2 Aerodrome-specific details
  • AD 2.3 Published operating hours
  • AD 2.4 Handling services and facilities
  • AD 2.5 Passenger facilities
  • AD 2.12 Runway physical characteristics
  • AD 2.24 Aerodrome charts

3. How does a change reach the AIP?

A learning model of the controlled information life cycle. Actual approval steps depend on State procedures.

Think like an auditor: Who supplied the data? Who checked it? What was published? When did it become effective? What later document changed it?

4. How does aviation information change?

Not every change is an AIP Amendment. Different products serve different purposes.

AIP AMDT

Lasting or permanent information changes.

Example: permanent airport data update
AIP SUP

Long temporary changes or short complex changes with extensive text/charts.

Example: complex temporary airport restriction
NOTAM

Time-sensitive operational change or condition.

Example: short-notice runway closure
AIC

Explanatory, advisory or forward-looking information.

Example: upcoming administrative change
AIRAC

Common effective-date system, not another AIP part.

28-day cycle for defined important changes

5. How AVIAENERGO should use the information

Keep the State’s published capability separate from company readiness and the actual flight decision.

01

Airport published data

Read GEN, ENR and AD plus current AMDT, SUP, NOTAM and charts. Find what is stated, not what you assume.

Example: H24 is a published schedule, not a full service guarantee.
02

Company capability

Check company scope, contract, partner, authorized staff, equipment, access, insurance and availability.

A service listed in AIP does not prove AVIAENERGO provides it.
03

Flight-specific readiness

Match the real aircraft, date, time, passengers, permissions, current notices and available resources.

The final decision remains with the authorized operational teams.
Simple training example — Sohag HESG

An aircraft is expected to stay overnight. Do not say “yes” because the airport is published H24. First check the applicable parking rules (the supplied archive includes SUP 43/2026), its current validity, airport permission, aircraft requirements and AVIAENERGO readiness. This is an example, not a current clearance.

7. Sources & important boundaries

Official basis, traceability and what this learning map does not prove.

Egypt-specific facts are taken from the Egyptian AIP Extra Regular AMDT 03/26 archive supplied in this project, notably GEN 0.1, GEN 3.1 and GEN 1.7. The AIP identifies the Ministry of Civil Aviation as issuing authority and NANSC AIS as the information service. It states that the English text governs where its Arabic and English meanings conflict.

Operational boundary: This learning map does not check current NOTAM, current AIRAC AMDT, current SUP checklist, the aircraft’s performance limitations, or actual company resources. It is an educational map and a basis for a controlled training system, not an official flight briefing or operating approval.
